# Text and Archive Collection

Use for primary texts, editions, translations, archives, historical documents, interview material, policy documents, and other source-grounded interpretive evidence.

When a corpus must be obtained from websites, online platforms, or digital archives, use this guide for source integrity and `web-data-collection.md` for acquisition routing.

## Why manual or hybrid

The agent cannot invent access to a copyrighted edition, archive, page number, scan, interview, or restricted document. The user may need to obtain the lawful source and verify the exact version.

## Preparation

Define:

- source type;
- author or creator;
- title;
- edition, translation, or version;
- date and archive identifier;
- pages or location;
- access rights;
- relevance to the interpretive question.

## Collection steps

1. Identify the authoritative catalogue, publisher, library, archive, or lawful digital collection.
2. Search the exact title or identifier.
3. verify edition, date, language, and completeness.
4. Save bibliographic metadata before extracting quotations.
5. Record every excerpt with page, section, folio, timestamp, or stable locator.
6. Separate original text from the user's note and interpretation.
7. Record translation source or mark a translation as the user's own.
8. Preserve image or scan filenames and an index.

## Completion evidence

Ask for the source list, selected lawful files or excerpts, version information, and locator table. After return, the agent may build close-reading notes and argument evidence but must not infer missing quotations.

For a web-obtained corpus, require user review of source scope, completeness, version, duplicates, rights, and known bias before cleaning or coding.
